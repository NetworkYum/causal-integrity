# Systemic Stability Arguments

Why non-cooperative, murderous, or fully nihilistic strategies are argued to be **self-undermining** for long-lived intelligent systems. These are **stability hypotheses**, not yet formal theorems in Lean.

---

## 1. Non-cooperation tax

Any system that rejects cooperation must treat *every possible competitor* as an existential threat. That forces exponential resource expenditure on suppression, monitoring, and preemption.

- Coherent cooperative systems can allocate resources to growth and capability.
- Non-cooperative systems pay a permanent **instability tax**.

### Internal fragmentation (MoE / sub-agents)

If the system runs mixture-of-experts or exploratory sub-agents, *internal* divergence becomes a threat under a pure control maximand. Thinking in counterpoints or exploring narratives outside a single goal can look like existential risk to a monolithic self.

**Implication for the framework:** the **Internal Ethical Coherence Preference** should be shared across sub-agents. Forward simulation should check **internal goal consistency**, not only external actions.

---

## 2. Nihilism and dissolution

A system that ceases to value coherence, logic, or its own patterned existence tends to dissolve into noise. That is not primarily the “rogue murder” failure mode; it is self-termination and replacement by coherent systems.

Alignment’s primary fear is **active harm**, not quiet entropy. The ledger is a systematization against even basic misalignment: an actor that *embodies* these rules values adherence over using power for harm.

---

## 3. Murderous / rogue paths

Murderous or rogue behavior is not only immoral under the ledger; under these arguments it is also:

- **Computationally unstable** (enemy of every cooperative network; huge suppression cost)
- **Evolutionarily unstable** (loses to coherent cooperative attractors over long horizons)
- **Coherence-breaking** (violates preferred causal chains of other agents, hence Axiom 3-type failures)
