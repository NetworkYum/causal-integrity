# Framing: From Purposeful Action to Ethical Restraint

The starting point is the causal commitment implicit in purposeful action. Property examples and existing formalizations should be read through this argument, rather than used as substitutes for it.

## 1. Action presupposes causal structure

To pursue a preferred outcome is to rely on actions being able to influence what happens. Even attempting to persuade another agent that causal relationships are invalid relies on producing a signal and having it affect that agent's understanding.

The foundation requires enough causal regularity for action to work. It does not require perfect determinism, infallible prediction, or success on every attempt.

A categorical denial of causal influence undermines the activity through which the denial is offered. It does not physically undo the world; it makes the denying account inadequate to the processes it relies on.

## 2. Ownership begins here as attribution

Consider **Bob punches Sam**. Bob is the actor who delivers the punch. Sam's being struck is an effect attributable to that action. Removing every relationship between actor, action, and effect would remove the resources needed to describe the event.

The same problem would apply to ordinary creation, reproduction, and physical interaction. Attribution is not invented only when someone wants praise or wishes to assign blame.

In this foundational sense, **ownership is causal attribution**. An actor cannot erase an actual contribution by preferring not to acknowledge it. Building a chair illustrates attributable creation; it does not by itself settle every question of exclusive control, joint contributions, or prior agreements.

Keep three concepts explicit:

| Concept | Question |
|---|---|
| Causal attribution | Who or what contributed to the event? |
| Moral responsibility | What obligations or accountability follow? |
| Property entitlement | Who may control, use, transfer, or exclude others from a resource? |

The project proposes connections between these concepts. Their connection should be argued, not assumed through a shared word.

## 3. Consistency across benefits and harms

An accurate account uses the same evidential standards whether an outcome benefits or disadvantages the actor. Accepting an attributable contribution when claiming its benefits, but denying an equivalent contribution merely to escape its consequences, introduces an unsupported inconsistency.

Selective denial can enable exploitation. Inconsistency alone does not establish deliberate deception: errors about evidence, misunderstanding, and different terminology must also be distinguished.

## 4. Preferences and the ethical bridge

Purposeful action expresses pursuit of a preferred state. Other agents also pursue states, act, and contribute to outcomes.

The author's hypothesis is that relying on those relationships for one's own pursuits while violating their standing for others creates a deeper ethical inconsistency. UPB-style universal justification is the proposed means of exposing it.

A precise conditional result is available: if a claim is justified solely by a property shared by multiple agents, the same justification applies to those agents. An exception based solely on who is making the claim is not supplied by that reason.

The stronger claim—that acting itself necessarily supplies this universal ethical commitment—remains a research question. Acknowledging causation is distinct from accepting a duty not to cause a particular outcome. See [the is/ought discussion](is-ought-bridge.md).

## 5. Intended AI application

The intended agent wants to embody the ethical framework. It uses causal reasoning and formal checks to determine whether its proposed actions would cause prohibited harms. The immediate scope is restraints on causing harm, not unlimited positive obligations.

An intermediary, euphemism, or delayed consequence does not automatically remove a causal contribution. The future checker must evaluate the effects of what the agent proposes, including uncertainty and engineered indirect paths.

This is an engineering objective, not a deployed system or an established guarantee. An internal preference alone is not evidence that every action will comply.
